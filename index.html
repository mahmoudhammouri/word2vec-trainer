How do computers read text?

Machine-learning models only understand numbers. Before a model can classify a review, search documents or chat with us, every piece of text must be turned into vectors. These interactive pages show four classic ways to do it — from the simplest to the one that learns meaning — step by step, with your own sentences in English or Arabic.

Method 1

One-Hot Encoding

Every word gets its own position. A word becomes a vector of zeros with a single 1.

cat → [0, 1, 0, 0, 0, 0] dog → [0, 0, 0, 0, 1, 0]
  • vocabulary & word IDs
  • one word = one vector
  • why it has no meaning
Open →Method 2

Bag of Words

A whole document becomes one vector of word counts. Word order is thrown away.

"the cat sat on the mat" → the:2 cat:1 sat:1 on:1 mat:1
  • counting words
  • document-term matrix
  • document similarity
Open →Method 3

TF-IDF

Counts are re-weighted: words frequent in one document but rare elsewhere score highest.

the → 0.00 (in every doc) mat → 0.30 (only in D1)
  • term frequency
  • inverse document frequency
  • finding keywords
Open →Method 4

Word2Vec

A neural network learns short, dense vectors so that words used in similar contexts end up close together.

cat → [ 0.82, -1.10, 0.35 ] dog → [ 0.79, -1.02, 0.41 ]
  • sliding window
  • Skip-gram & CBOW
  • training step by step
