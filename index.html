The training sentences

Word2Vec learns only from text. It never sees a definition — it learns a word's meaning from the words around it. These sentences are our whole training corpus.

Corpus

    Pre-processing

    1. Lower-case every word and drop punctuation.
    2. Split each sentence into words (tokens).
    3. Remove stop words such as the, a, in — they appear next to everything, so they carry little meaning. They are shown crossed out.
    Notice the patterns: king and queen share the words rules, kingdom, wears, crown; cat and dog share drinks, milk, water, chases. Those shared neighbours are exactly what the model will pick up.
    2

    Vocabulary & one-hot vectors

    Every distinct word gets an ID. A neural network needs numbers, so each word is fed in as a one-hot vector: a list of V zeros with a single 1 at the word's ID. One-hot vectors say nothing about meaning — every pair of words is equally different. Training will replace them with dense vectors.

    Training settings

    Choose the architecture and the hyper-parameters. Applying new settings rebuilds the training pairs and starts the network again from new random weights.

    Size of the hidden layer = length of every word vector. With N = 2 the weights are plotted directly; above 2 the space is projected to 2-D with PCA.
    How many words on each side of the center word count as context.
    How big each weight update is. Too high → unstable, too low → slow.
    One epoch = one pass over all training pairs.
